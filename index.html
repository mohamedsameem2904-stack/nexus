Nº 7 · The Heritage

Materials with a Memory

Celluloid, that most sensuous of early plastics, is crafted in small batches following recipes from the 1920s. Its depth catches light like no modern resin can — a living material that ages with the writer’s hand.

Each 18k gold nib is cut, shaped, and polished by a single artisan over three days. The tipping is iridium‑osmium, hand‑ground to a buttery smoothness that varies subtly from pen to pen — a signature no machine can replicate.

We source oak from sustainably managed forests in the Jura, seasoned for seven years before it becomes a desk or a display case. Brass fittings are left unlacquered, developing a patina that tells the story of its use.

This is not stationery. It is a tool for the unhurried — for letters, for journals, for thoughts that deserve the weight of ink on paper.
